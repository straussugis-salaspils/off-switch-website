# Do you need to find the root cause before a reaction can change?

Someone finds a memory that genuinely starts the response.

Instead of working with it, they ask:

> But this cannot be the root. What happened first?

The scene feels too recent, too small or insufficiently dramatic to explain everything happening now.

So the search continues for the real cause: earliest, deepest and grand enough to make the whole biography click into one elegant diagram.

I take a simpler view. I do not need to prove a perfect causal history before the work begins. I need accessible material and a response that can be tested.

## The first memory found need not be the first event

What appears first in the work is not necessarily the earliest episode in life.

It is the scene the person can imagine now and to which a specific response exists: yesterday's conversation, a dismissal two years ago, an examination or a parent's words.

A later date does not make the episode useless. If imagining it starts the response, we have a working target that can be checked before and after the work.

Whether it was historically first can wait.

## The final drop did not create the whole glass

I use an imaginary numerical metaphor.

Suppose someone could carry 3.9 out of 5 but not 3.91. A tiny new situation arrived after prolonged overload and became the final drop.

This is not a measurement scale or universal mathematics. It distinguishes the last visible event from what accumulated before it.

**The final drop did not create the whole glass.**

But it can still be an accessible point of entry. Work with it and test it—without declaring it the sole source of the entire state.

If connected material continues to start the response afterwards, the work did not end with that drop.

## One perfect root may not exist

Sometimes a first fright is relatively clear. Yet a series of later events may have repeated and strengthened the fear.

Other patterns are less tidy: an anxious background, overload, conflict, expectation of another conflict, then one small situation that crosses the threshold.

Which one is the root? The earliest, most dramatic or latest event?

Choosing the most attractive candidate does not prove that it alone maintains today's response.

I am not claiming root causes never exist. I am saying that finding one perfect cause is not a prerequisite for changing a response.

## I begin with material available now

If someone cannot remember their whole past, I do not extract a full biography by force.

The first need is confidence that when difficult material appears, they can manage the response.

There is no first target we must guess correctly. There is a process: learn to manage the present state, choose one accessible scene and work with a response that can be produced and tested.

Accessible does not mean unimportant. It means real work can begin there instead of another year of searching for the ideal explanation.

## The most severe memory need not come first

Some people think serious work should begin with the worst possible memory.

I regard that as poor sequence rather than bravery.

In my work, a person rates the intensity of a response from zero to five. They first gain experience with accessible material—roughly, the threes—then fours, then fives.

This is my working sequence, not a universal clinical scale.

The point is that the most difficult material is taken when the person can work with it without falling into it or depending entirely on my presence.

Gradual work is not a way to avoid the central material forever. It is how the person becomes able to work when they reach it.

## Testing matters more than a beautiful explanation

Suppose we find a scene that appears to explain everything. The person links the manager with a parent, today's fear with a school incident and the whole structure with one large theme.

Good. Now test the response.

After the work, the person imagines the original scene and deliberately checks for the old state.

If the reaction remains, the explanation did not complete the work. If it does not, we have a result for that target—even if we never proved it was the first event in life.

I do not need a flawless causal version. I need a result that can be examined.

## Start with what is accessible. Then look closely at what remains

We begin with the visible, accessible material, then test what still needs attention.

If the general intensity falls but one intonation, phrase or neighbouring scene still starts the response, more precise work begins. We did not need to predict that final element; testing revealed it.

## One neutral scene is not the entire chain

The opposite mistake is possible too.

One neutral scene is a result, not the whole biography. A similar response later may indicate another linked element, a signal previously missed, or a genuinely new event.

We do not decide which by loyalty to the old explanation. We take the actual situation and test what starts now.

The criterion remains clear: we do not collect memories for quantity. We continue while a specific response continues to be reproducible.

## The root need not be proved; the response needs to be tested

The final test is not in the past.

The person imagines the original memory, then a similar future, and eventually meets the relevant situation in real life.

If the old response does not start and another action is available, that is a result. If something remains, it is information about the next element—not a reason to defend the theory or discard every previous result.

The Method does not reconstruct objective historical truth. I cannot prove that one scene created everything that followed, and nobody needs to remember a complete chronology before earning the right to change.

I begin with what is accessible, test the specific response and follow what genuinely remains. The final answer comes neither from the elegance of the story nor the age of the memory.

It comes from life.

# When you no longer need to keep investigating the past

Near the end of the work, I sometimes have a strange conversation.

I ask, “What else bothers you? Where does a specific emotion still appear?”

We examine work, relationships, parents, money and major events. I continue asking questions, sometimes for half an hour.

The person cannot find another subject.

Not because they refuse to speak or have decided to forget everything. They remember their life. There is simply no next story that genuinely catches.

Then comes a reasonable question: *Should we search harder?*

Not always.

If the former response cannot be produced, life confirms the change and there is no new specific target, work with the past can end.

## Completion is not merely being tired of talking

I describe completion simply:

> It does not trigger, and there is no desire to discuss it.

Neither half can be discarded.

Someone may avoid a subject because it is painful, frightening or exhausting. They may say, “I do not care,” while their body has already tightened. That is reluctance—not completion.

After completed work, the person can remember and name the event without the former response starting. There is no reason to grind the same conversation for the hundredth time.

They do not avoid discussing it because the pain is too great.

**They are uninterested because there is nothing left to process there.**

## The memory remains; the old response does not

Completion is not amnesia.

The event occurred. The person remembers it and need not call it good, fair or useful. Nobody is required to forgive everyone involved or thank life for the educational opportunity.

The formula can be: “This happened to me. I remember it. It no longer catches me.”

The fact remains. What leaves is the old emotional charge that automatically imported that history into today.

Before the work, someone remembers a conversation from ten years ago and becomes angry, frightened or defensive now. Afterwards, the same event is remembered and may still be judged unacceptable, but it no longer commands the body and present behaviour.

That is the result—not a polished new narrative or correct vocabulary about acceptance.

## One zero does not complete an entire life

If one scene moves from four out of five to zero, that is a result for the scene—not the whole biography or every future conflict.

I do not proclaim total freedom from the past after one target. But neither should a neutral scene be treated as unreal merely because neighbouring areas still need checking.

One target is complete. The next question is whether another exists.

## Remembering every event is not required

Caution can produce the opposite extreme: *What if I forgot something? Should I review every episode of my life?*

Then the work becomes an indefinite biographical inventory.

My approach does not require every breakfast, school break and unpleasant phrase. We begin with key difficult histories the person remembers, then check broader areas where active material often remains. The criterion is not archive completeness but response.

If something genuinely catches, there is another target. If it does not, absence of a memory is not an emergency requiring the invention of a problem.

I do not search the past for the sake of the past. I search for what still governs the present.

## Bulldozer first, scalpel second

My metaphor is: bulldozer first, scalpel second.

Begin with the main accessible material—known difficult memories and themes. Much of the pattern may leave with the broader background.

Then become precise. What remains? Which look, phrase, situation or imagined future still starts a response?

If a specific remainder exists, use the scalpel. But the sequence does not require us to find something for the scalpel merely so the process looks serious and long.

Sometimes careful testing shows that nothing remains.

## I search for another target only while one exists

Near completion, I do not ask once, “Everything fine?” and accept a quick yes.

I keep looking. What still brings fear, anger, resentment, guilt or another specific response? What happens when the person imagines an important future situation?

Sometimes a target appears. Good—the work continues. Sometimes a broad label conceals one scene. We take the scene.

And sometimes, after systematic work, half an hour of questions produces nothing that genuinely starts the old state.

The person does not owe the professional a new problem so the meeting feels worthwhile.

The absence of another reproducible target is also a result.

## Actions show more than words

Words alone are not sufficient evidence. I look at life.

The person enters a conversation they previously avoided. They can choose instead of agreeing automatically. They return to an action that was unavailable. The performance need not be perfect, but the old response no longer removes the possibility of acting differently.

A questionnaire describes a state. A rating tracks a particular response. Action demonstrates life.

If someone says the work is complete and the old situation fully controls them the next day, something remains to be examined. If the response cannot be produced, a similar future is neutral and life confirms a new action, the result exists outside the meeting.

## When there is nothing left to find, work with the past ends

There is no single magic sign for everyone. I consider several things together:

- the old response cannot be reproduced;
- the memory remains but no longer catches;
- another action is available in similar situations;
- any remainder has been taken as a new target;
- no further real target can be found.

My central work ends when that list ends.

The list is not a complete inventory of human life. It contains what still produces a response and requires work.

Stopping while such a target exists is too early. Continuing to excavate after it no longer exists serves the process rather than the person.

## Completion does not remove future emotions

The person does not become stone. There will be new conflict, loss, mistakes and people with objectionable voices.

A new emotion does not prove the old work returned. A specific new episode needs its own assessment: an old remainder, a new event or a proportionate response to what is happening now.

Completion of work with the past does not cancel life. It means the old material no longer has to govern every new day.

Then attention can move elsewhere: new actions, relationships, work, rules and a life that needs building rather than further excavation.

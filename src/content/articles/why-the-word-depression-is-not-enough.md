# Why the word ‘depression’ is not enough to show what is happening

A person joins a session and says, “I have depression.”

I hear that life is heavy. But for the work I do, the word is still too large.

It is like telling a mechanic, “The car will not move.” Important information; the screwdriver does not yet know where to go. The engine may have failed, the fuel may be gone, a wheel may be elsewhere, or somebody has parked a concrete wall in front and proposed treating it together with the motor.

Depression may be a real medical diagnosis. I do not make or remove that diagnosis in an article. My narrower point is that one large word does not provide one ready-made target for my work.

## One label can cover the entire field

Beneath the word may sit several things at once: painful memories, bodily heaviness, fear of the future, low energy, paralysis, loss of direction and the thought that it will always be this way.

The label does not make the state unreal. Nor does renaming it “an exciting growth opportunity” improve matters.

The practical problem is that a mixed package cannot be changed through one vague operation. We need to see what is firing.

What looks like one grey wall may be several different lines working in parallel, each adding its own weight.

## Past, present and future may all be active

One line points backwards: a failed relationship, dismissal, betrayal, humiliation or a conversation still replaying internally.

Another is present now: heaviness, no desire, uncertainty, low energy or being unable to begin a simple task.

A third is an imagined future:

- “I will never find decent work again.”
- “Nothing this good will happen again.”
- “At my age, everything is over.”

That future has not occurred, but the internal film already has lighting, a soundtrack and a very committed cast.

Then comes a fourth line: the response to the response. The person feels bad and adds, “This will never end,” “Nothing can help,” or “Something is fundamentally wrong with me.”

I use deliberately unceremonious names for these two levels: the stuff, and the stuff about the stuff. The second layer does not solve the first. It adds another response to it.

## You do not need a perfect map before beginning

The obvious next mistake is to think every element and cause must be found before any work can start.

When several heavy things are active, the person does not need a doctoral thesis about their own state. They need a way to work with what has surfaced now.

A memory appears: notice the response. Bodily heaviness rises: notice it. A black picture of the future appears: notice it. “Nothing will work” arrives: treat it as another active element, not as a divine memorandum.

First, the person learns to interrupt the current response and return to a more manageable state. The task is not to process an entire biography by bedtime. It is to stop drowning in each wave as it appears.

The work can then become systematic.

## One accessible element, then the next

I approach this technically. What is at the surface? We take it, rate its intensity from zero to five, work, and test again.

What remains? What appears next?

One episode may reveal a neighbour. Beneath a bodily sensation may sit an image of the future. When fear falls, helplessness may become visible. Sometimes much of the state is maintained not by an event but by the conclusion that it will last forever.

We do not need to guess the ideal order in advance. We work with available targets and test the change.

I sometimes describe the early stage as a bulldozer: clear the main heavy material. Then comes the scalpel: identify and test what remains. This is not a medical model of depression. It is a way to avoid pretending that one operation can be performed on a whole label.

## Removing uncertainty does not create confidence

This is an important limit of the Method.

Suppose uncertainty falls to zero. The uncertainty is no longer active—but confidence does not have to appear by itself.

The technique removes. It does not create.

You can reduce fear of action and still lack the skill. You can stop falling into an old memory and still not know what to do tomorrow. You can reduce the weight of a former role without receiving a new profession, relationship or purpose.

**Zero clears the room. Furnishing it remains a separate job.**

## Sometimes the old life really did end

Not everything is an automatic response. A job may genuinely be gone. Health may change what is possible. A relationship may have ended. Retirement may close a role that organised decades of life.

No emotional technique restores a position that no longer exists. But beside the fact can sit an additional construction: “If that role ended, my whole life ended.”

The fact remains. The conclusion about a permanently empty future can be examined separately.

When its charge reduces, another question becomes possible: what needs to be built now?

## A quieter state does not fill an empty calendar

Removing fear, resentment and painful memories may make the inner world calmer. An empty calendar does not acquire content as a result.

That stage is construction:

- Which actions still matter?
- What does the person want to create?
- Where is their competence useful now?
- What gives energy or a sense of movement?
- Which relationship, personal or professional direction deserves a real experiment?

I do not supply somebody with meaning in an elegant box. Convenient, perhaps, but it would amount to selling other people’s lives.

Meaning can begin like a closed box. You do not yet know exactly what is inside, but you can test its weight and notice that it is not empty. Interest, energy, a direction and a six-month experiment may arrive before the final job title does.

Life reveals the contents through action.

## The result does not fit back into one word

I test several kinds of change: can the person interrupt a current response, have specific memories and future images reduced, remain steadier between meetings, see plans again and take real actions?

One person’s state may contain fewer lines, another’s more. A medical issue, physical exhaustion, continuing danger or a real loss may sit alongside them. Changing several automatic responses is not the same as removing a diagnosis.

If you may be depressed, seek assessment from a qualified medical or mental-health professional. If you are thinking about harming yourself or cannot keep yourself safe, contact local emergency or crisis services now and involve a trusted person who can stay with you. This Method is not crisis care or a substitute for appropriate treatment.

My work has a more specific aim. The undivided weight becomes distinguishable. Past episodes, present sensations, imagined futures and the response to the response can be examined separately.

Then, as old pressure falls, we can see what life actually lacks: a skill, a plan, a role, a relationship or meaning.

The word “depression” matters. It is simply not enough to decide which specific response or life task comes next.

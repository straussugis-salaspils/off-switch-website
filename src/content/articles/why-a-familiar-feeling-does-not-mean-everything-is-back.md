# Why a familiar feeling does not always mean the old problem is back

Someone worked with a specific response. When it was tested again, the old response could not be produced. In life, the person acted differently too.

Time passed.

Then a manager used an unpleasant tone and everything tightened inside again.

The first thought was, *It is all back.* The second usually arrives immediately: *Then all the previous work was pointless.*

I would not rush into either conclusion.

A similar feeling does not prove that the same response has returned. My first question is less dramatic:

> What exactly happened now?

## I declare neither failure nor victory in advance

There are two equally inconvenient errors.

The first is to say that any familiar emotion proves the Method failed and the old response returned.

The second is to defend the previous result: “No, that cannot have happened. You must be feeling it incorrectly.”

Neither is useful.

I do not erase a tested result merely because today's response resembles it. Nor do I protect a theory from current facts.

If the person feels bad again, there is a present episode to examine. Which person, phrase, tone and moment started which precise response?

The general label may be the same—fear, anger, tension—while the starting signal is different.

## In my model, a specific completed response does not return

I say this directly: when a specific old response has been completed, it cannot return.

Every word matters. I mean a particular response that was actually worked through and tested—not the person's whole biography, every future manager, all dogs, every conflict and every loss.

This is my working model, not a medical guarantee for all people and all circumstances.

The person does not become invulnerable. Life signs no agreement to stop producing unpleasant events.

There may be another signal from the old situation, another element in a wider theme, or a completely new event that creates a new response. All three can sound like, “I am frightened again,” while being different work.

## One zero belongs to one specific target

Suppose someone imagines one conflict. The response is four out of five. After the work, they deliberately return to that scene and receive zero.

That is a genuine result for that target.

It is not automatically zero for the whole biography, every category of people and every neighbouring signal. But a later episode does not make the earlier result false retroactively.

The tested scene may remain neutral. Something else may be starting now.

## Sometimes the content is neutral but the tone remains active

Someone may have worked with a manager's criticism: the words, content and look. The old response ends and a later critical conversation is manageable.

Six months later, another manager says almost the same thing and tension appears.

It is tempting to conclude that fear of criticism returned. But perhaps the content is not the active signal. The particular intonation may start a separate response that was never tested.

“I am afraid of my manager again” is a broad label. Precise examination may show that the manager, criticism and earlier words are neutral; one tone is active.

Then we work with what started now rather than repeatedly polishing a scene that remains neutral.

## Sometimes another part of a wider theme appears

One completed scene can belong to a larger subject.

Someone may be calm with one manager and one form of criticism, but react to another person's look, pause or phrase. This need not mean the first episode returned. Neighbouring material may simply have become visible.

There is no need to turn it into another elaborate theory—one look means one parent, a pause means the other, and the tone explains the entire family tree.

A specific signal producing a specific response is already enough to begin a test. Work with what exists now, not with a theory about what should have returned.

## Sometimes life creates a genuinely new event

Imagine someone who previously feared dogs. The old response was completed and tested: imagined dogs, real dogs, even approaching a German shepherd without the former fear.

Then a new aggressive dog bites them.

The bite is a new event, with its own consequences.

A new response can develop after a new event. In my model, that is new material, not the return of the old completed scene.

Earlier work should not make someone insensitive to actual danger, and it should not require pretending the bite did not occur.

Treat the new event as new.

## I test the current episode instead of defending the old theory

When someone says, “It has all returned,” I return to facts:

- What happened now?
- What was the exact starting moment?
- Was it the words, tone, look, pause, action or the new situation itself?
- What did the person feel?
- Does earlier material appear, or is the event experienced as new?

We may need to divide the episode into parts and test again. The direction remains simple: work with what actually starts now, then test that target.

## If something triggers, it is a gift

I often tell clients:

> If something triggers, it is a gift.

The gift is not feeling bad again. Nobody needs to thank the manager for a vicious tone or the dog for its teeth.

The gift is a specific signal that can now be tested. Instead of “Sometimes I feel anxious again,” there is an episode, moment and response. We know where to look.

## The next step depends on what is found

If the tone remains active, work with the tone. If another old element appears, test the connected material. If a new event occurred, work with the new response. If no response can be produced, do not manufacture a problem because one was expected.

The word *returned* can collapse a completed scene, a neighbouring signal, a wider theme and today's new event into one heap.

I separate the heap.

A familiar emotion is not evidence that the same response returned. The earlier result should not be cancelled without examination—or protected from new facts.

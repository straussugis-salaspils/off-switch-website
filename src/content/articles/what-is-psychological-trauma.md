# What psychological trauma is—and what continues in the present

The event ended ten years ago. The person knows the date and place and can explain calmly why it happened.

Then someone uses a similar tone of voice—and the present disappears.

The body tightens. The voice vanishes. The person defends, leaves, agrees or attacks before there has been time to decide what to do.

In my working model, the central distinction is this: the danger or difficult event has ended, while a response connected with it continues to operate now.

But people often use the word *trauma* for four different things at once. That makes it difficult to see what exactly needs attention.

## The event, the memory and the response are not the same thing

First, there is what actually happened.

Second, there is how the event was preserved in memory. Memory is not a video recording. Two people can remember one occasion differently and be entirely certain of their respective versions.

I use a simple family story about Aunt Zhenya. One person is sure she attended an old family event; another is equally sure she did not. Who is right? Thirty years later, nobody knows.

Third, there is the meaning attached to the remembered scene: *I was not loved; I am weak; mistakes are punished; I cannot speak around people like this.*

Fourth, there is the automatic reaction today.

```text
event
≠ remembered scene
≠ meaning attached to the scene
≠ response that starts today
```

This does not mean the facts of the past are unimportant or that the person invented what happened. It means changing today's response does not always require winning a historical court case about every detail.

I need to see what the remembered scene is doing now.

## Not every difficult event becomes a continuing reaction

Someone can experience a severe event, remember it and retain choice in similar situations later.

Another person can spend years reacting to a particular voice, look, smell, place or facial expression after an event that appeared less dramatic from the outside.

The visible scale of the event does not measure today's response. The reverse also matters: a strong reaction does not prove that the present situation is safe.

If a manager is genuinely humiliating people, that is a present fact. If a partner repeatedly breaks agreements, it should not be filed under *memory* because that makes the analysis tidier. A real problem requires a real response.

I separate four questions:

- What has ended?
- What continues to start automatically?
- What is genuinely happening today?
- Which skill or action is required now?

A clinical diagnosis is a separate question. It cannot be established from one memory—or from this article.

## Why a safe present does not always convince the reaction

You may understand that today's dog is friendly. The unfortunate golden retriever has done absolutely nothing wrong.

Yet if the image of a dog is linked with an earlier fright, the response can begin before the breed and temperament have been reviewed.

The same applies beyond fear. A neutral pause can feel like rejection. Disagreement can register as attack. One mistake can become a verdict on the entire person.

The logic of the present may be correct: *This person is not that person. I am an adult now. The danger is over.* But a knee reflex is not cancelled by deciding that the knee should remain still.

In my working model, a specific signal today can press a response button connected with earlier scenes. This is an authorial model, not a claim that every intense emotion is trauma.

## What it means to work with psychological trauma

For me, the work is not a search for one perfect explanation of an entire life, nor an immediate descent into the most difficult memory.

The working unit is a specific response that continues today.

First, the person needs to bring the current state back within a manageable range. Then we can work separately with accessible scenes and check what remains active.

Two distinctions matter throughout:

- completing one response does not mean the entire history is complete;
- understanding why a response began does not mean the response itself has changed.

The work becomes much safer and more precise when neither distinction is sacrificed for an impressive story.

## What remains when the reaction reduces

The memory can remain. So can the person's judgement of what happened.

Nobody is required to call violence good, decide that a harmful manager was admirable or reconcile with what remains unacceptable.

What can leave is the obligation to relive the old response whenever the present resembles the past.

Then separate tasks become visible:

- learning to speak;
- setting a boundary;
- leaving a relationship;
- changing working conditions;
- building something that did not exist before.

Emotional work restores choice. It does not choose on the person's behalf.

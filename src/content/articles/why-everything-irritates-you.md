# Why everything irritates you

A message arrives at the wrong moment. A child asks a question. A colleague places a cup down too loudly. Someone walks slowly in front of you.

Each event is small; the answer is almost identical: *Everyone leave me alone.*

When one person irritates you, that relationship can be examined. When everything irritates you, I do not begin with the last cup.

I look at how much was already on the scale before it arrived.

## The final small thing rarely explains the whole response

I use a rough metaphor. Someone may be carrying four out of five. Another 0.01 arrives and the system no longer holds.

This is not a medical scale. It illustrates a threshold.

**The final message did not create the overload. It entered a system with no free space.**

Look only at the last event and the response seems absurd. Count the whole background and it may contain:

- older irritation still being carried;
- recent conflicts continuing internally;
- lack of sleep and present overload;
- constant switching and no silence;
- fear appearing externally as sharpness;
- a real environment demanding too much.

The world need not have become worse. The space between signal and response may simply have almost disappeared.

## “It is my personality” is an early conclusion

If someone lived with themselves calmly for thirty years and every sound has irritated them for six months, I would not rush to announce a new personality.

Character is a convenient explanation: *I am simply angry.* Investigation complete.

But a repeated response amplified by overload can look like a trait. The same mess produces mild annoyance on one day and an explosion on another. The mess did not change; the background did.

I would not assign today's cup responsibility for the entire event before checking which prepared button it pressed.

This remains a hypothesis. Someone may genuinely live among people crossing their boundaries. Present facts are not cancelled.

## Irritation at everything and anger towards one person are different tasks

A specific manager producing five clear episodes is a particular chain of anger. It may extend to similar authority figures, but it is not “everything irritates me.”

If the manager, children, traffic, music, notifications and one's own shirt appear to have joined a conspiracy, examine the wider load.

Two parts must remain distinct:

1. Emotional material maintaining tension.
2. A life containing no minute without somebody else's request.

Remove only the response and twenty notifications per hour remain bad organization. Turn off notifications while five conflicts continue internally and quiet may still not feel quiet.

## What I do with this state

I do not begin with “stop being irritated.” That is another demand addressed to someone with no space left.

First, reduce the present state enough to restore an interval between signal and action.

Then identify the strongest accessible lines: a conflict, fear, repeating person, persistent scene or threatening future. There is no need for an encyclopaedia of everything that ever went badly.

In parallel, examine the day:

- When is the person genuinely unavailable?
- Is any time protected?
- Are important tasks done—or only the loudest requests served?
- Is the volume physically possible for one person?

An emotional technique cannot hire another employee or make a family respect a boundary never expressed.

## What the result looks like

Not gratitude towards the neighbour drilling at seven in the morning.

Irritants become different again. Real rudeness receives a clear assessment and response. A small delay remains small. Fatigue is noticed before everyone becomes an enemy. The person can say no, close a door, change the schedule or have the conversation without first exploding.

The honest test is the next period of load. If everything again becomes equally irritating, some background, way of living or objectively impossible system remains unchanged.
